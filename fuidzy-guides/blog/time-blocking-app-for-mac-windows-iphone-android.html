Time blocking app for Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android

By the Fuidzy team · Updated 2026-10-09

If you use more than one device, a browser-based planner is the simplest way to time block everywhere. Native apps feel smoother on their own platform; the web works on all of them. Pick based on where you spend your day.

The cross-device problem

Most people plan on a laptop and check the plan on a phone. If your time blocking app only lives on one, the plan drifts out of date. So the real question isn't 'which app is best' but 'where do I actually look at my day'.

Your options

  • Calendar apps (Apple Calendar, Google Calendar) work everywhere and can hold blocks, but they're built for events, not tasks.
  • Native planner apps feel fast and integrate with the operating system, but are usually tied to one platform or priced per platform.
  • Browser-based planners run on any device with a browser, so the plan is the same on Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android.

What to check

  1. Does it sync reliably between the devices you use?
  2. Can you add a task in a few seconds on a phone?
  3. Does it support dragging blocks, not just typing times?
  4. What's the free tier, and does it have limits you'll hit this week?
  5. Can you export or leave if you change your mind?

Time blocking in Apple Calendar or Google Calendar

  1. Create a calendar called 'Blocks' so it can be shown or hidden.
  2. Add each block as an event with a clear name, like 'Deep work: report'.
  3. Use colour for block types: focus, admin, recovery.
  4. Turn on a notification for the start of each focus block.
  5. Drag events to reschedule. Review each evening.

It works, but tasks live elsewhere, so you copy items between the list and the calendar by hand. Planners that treat a task and its time slot as one thing remove that step.

A cross-device routine that sticks

  • Plan on the device you use for deep work, usually a laptop.
  • Glance at the plan on your phone, and only edit there for small moves.
  • Pick one place for capture and always use it.
  • Do a two-minute evening review on whichever device is closest.

Planning this on a real timeline is easier than reading about it.

Troubleshooting sync problems

  • Check you're signed in to the same account on every device.
  • Reload the page or app after a long gap offline.
  • Avoid editing the same block on two devices at once.
  • If a plan looks wrong, trust the device that was edited last and re-check the other.

What to look for on a phone specifically

  • A layout that works with one thumb, not a shrunken desktop screen.
  • Fast capture: one tap to add, one line to type.
  • Readable timelines at small sizes: you should still be able to see the shape of the day.
  • Reasonable behaviour with a patchy connection.
