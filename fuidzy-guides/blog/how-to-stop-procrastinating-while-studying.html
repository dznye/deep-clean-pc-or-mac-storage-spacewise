How to stop procrastinating while studying (what actually works)

By the Fuidzy team · Updated 2026-10-09

Procrastination is usually about avoiding a feeling, not about laziness. Make the first step tiny, remove the easiest distractions, work in short timed sessions, and decide tomorrow's first task tonight.

Why you procrastinate

Students often put off studying because the task feels big, vague or likely to reveal that they don't understand something. Avoiding it relieves that feeling briefly, which makes avoidance feel like the right move. The way out is to make starting so small and specific that it stops feeling threatening.

Seven things that work

  1. Shrink the start. "Open the textbook to page 40" beats "study chemistry". Aim to begin for just five minutes.
  2. Make it specific. Name the exact task and outcome: "do questions 1 to 10".
  3. Work in short timed sessions. Use 25 minutes and a break, as in the Pomodoro technique.
  4. Remove friction for studying and add it for distractions. Phone in another room, blocker on, materials ready.
  5. Schedule it, with a time. A task with a slot happens far more often than one on a list.
  6. Start with the easy win. A quick task builds momentum for the hard one.
  7. Decide tomorrow's first task tonight. Mornings go better when nothing needs deciding.

When the material is the problem

Sometimes you avoid a subject because you don't understand it. In that case, the fix isn't willpower; it's help. Try an easier explanation, ask a classmate or tutor, or start with the simplest piece. Confusion is a reason to ask, not a character flaw.

Build a setup that makes it easier

  • A consistent place and time for studying.
  • A visible plan for the day, with start times.
  • A reward you actually care about after a session.
  • A friend or study group for accountability.

What to do when you slip

You will slip. Don't punish yourself; restart with the smallest next step. Treat a lost afternoon as information about your plan: was the task too big, the time wrong, or the environment bad? Fix that, not yourself.

A ten-minute start routine

  1. Clear your desk of everything except what you need.
  2. Put your phone out of reach.
  3. Write the single task for this session on a sticky note.
  4. Set a timer for 25 minutes.
  5. Begin with the easiest part of the task.

Common excuses and what to do instead

The excuseA better move
I'll start when I feel motivatedStart for five minutes; motivation often follows action
It's too bigPick one page or ten questions
I need to organise my notes firstStudy with what you have; tidy later
I'm too tiredDo a short low-effort task, then rest properly

Plan around low-energy times

If afternoons are your slump, don't schedule your hardest material there. Put light tasks in low-energy slots and save the demanding ones for when you're sharp. Fighting your own rhythm is a quiet cause of a lot of procrastination.

Common questions

Why do I procrastinate when studying?

Often to avoid discomfort: the task feels big, unclear or reveals gaps. Making the first step tiny and specific helps.

How do I start studying when I don't feel like it?

Commit to five minutes on one specific task. Starting usually builds momentum.

Does the Pomodoro technique help with procrastination?

Many people find short timed sessions make starting easier because the commitment is small.

How can I stop using my phone while studying?

Put it in another room or use a blocker, and keep short phone breaks for scheduled breaks.

Is procrastination a sign of laziness?

Usually not. It's more often a way of avoiding an unpleasant feeling. Specific planning and support help.
