How to study for finals: a plan for the last two weeks

By the Fuidzy team · Updated 2026-10-09

For finals, prioritise by weight and weakness, practise instead of re-reading, space your review, and keep sleep and meals in the plan. If you only have a week, cut scope rather than cram everything.

First, triage

With finals close, you can't do everything, so choose. List each exam with its date and how much it counts toward your grade, then rate your confidence in each topic. Spend most of your time where weight is high and confidence is low. Topics you already know well need only a quick refresh.

Study by doing, not by re-reading

Re-reading feels productive and mostly isn't. Better: close the book and try to recall the material, do practice problems, work through past papers, and explain topics aloud. Details are in our guide to the active recall study method.

A two-week plan

DaysFocus
1 to 3Map the syllabus, gather past papers, fix the biggest gaps
4 to 8Topic-by-topic practice, weakest first, short review at the end of each day
9 to 11Timed past papers, mark honestly, patch the errors
12 to 13Consolidate: summaries, flash recall, easy wins
14Light review, early night, logistics sorted

If you only have a week

  1. Pick the topics most likely to be examined and worth the most marks.
  2. Do one timed past paper on day one to learn where you stand.
  3. Work in two or three focused sessions a day with real breaks.
  4. Review each evening for ten minutes.
  5. Accept that you won't cover everything, and decide what to skip on purpose.

When you're burnt out

If you're exhausted, a good night's sleep and a lighter day often beat another grim six hours. Shrink the plan: pick two or three essential tasks a day and do them well. If you feel overwhelmed for more than a few days, talk to someone, such as a tutor, a counsellor or a friend. This is general advice, not medical advice.

The night before and the morning of

  • Don't learn new material. Skim your summaries.
  • Pack everything you need, and plan your route and timing.
  • Eat properly and sleep. A tired brain recalls worse.
  • In the exam, read every question first and start with what you know.

Tips by subject type

Subject typeWhat works best
Maths and sciencesWork through problems; redo the ones you got wrong without looking at the solution
Essay subjectsPlan essay outlines from memory, then write one timed essay per topic
Memorisation-heavyFlashcards and recall, spaced over days

Making the most of past papers

  1. Do the first one early, under timed conditions, to see where you stand.
  2. Mark it honestly using the mark scheme.
  3. Make an error log: the question, the mistake, the fix.
  4. Redo similar questions a few days later without notes.
  5. Do the last paper close to the exam to rehearse timing.

Common questions

How do I study for finals in one week?

Triage by weight and weakness, do a timed past paper first, work in short focused sessions, and review each evening. Cut scope instead of cramming everything.

How many hours should I study per day for finals?

Enough to do focused work without exhausting yourself. Several focused hours with breaks beats a full day of distracted desk time.

What's the best way to study for finals?

Practice retrieval: past papers, practice problems and recall, with spaced review rather than re-reading.

Should I pull an all-nighter?

Usually not. Sleep supports memory and focus, so a rested short session tends to beat a tired long one.

What if I'm too stressed to study?

Shrink the plan, take a proper break, and talk to someone you trust. If stress is severe, seek professional support.
