AI study planner: build a revision schedule that survives exam week

By the Fuidzy team · Updated 2026-10-09

A good study plan works backwards from the exam date, splits topics into short sessions, and leaves buffer for the days that go wrong. AI can do the splitting; you decide the real hours.

Start from the date, not the syllabus

Most revision plans fail because they start with "I'll do a chapter a day" instead of counting the days you really have. Count first: how many days until each exam, minus days you can't study, minus a buffer. That number is your real budget, and it is almost always smaller than it feels.

The method

  1. List every exam or deadline with its date and weight.
  2. List the topics for each, and mark the ones you're weakest on.
  3. Ask the AI to split topics into 30 to 50 minute sessions and spread them, weakest first.
  4. Add review sessions: a quick revisit two or three days after first learning a topic.
  5. Leave one catch-up day per week for everything that slipped.

A prompt for the draft

My exams are: [subjects and dates]. I can study [hours] on weekdays and [hours] on weekends. My weakest topics are [list]. Build a day-by-day revision plan in 45 minute sessions, spaced so each topic is revisited a few days later, with one catch-up day each week.

Why spaced review matters

Revisiting material shortly before you'd forget it is the idea behind flashcard apps, and it generally beats re-reading everything the night before. Build short review slots into the plan rather than treating revision as one long final push. Even ten minutes of recall a few days after learning something helps it stick.

A sample week

DayPlan
MonWeak topic A (45 min), topic B (45 min), review of last week's topic
TuePractice questions on topic A, topic C (45 min)
WedWeak topic D, 10-minute recall of Monday
ThuPast paper, timed (60 min), mark and note errors
FriCatch-up day: whatever slipped
SatTwo focused sessions, then a proper break
SunLight review and plan next week

Common mistakes

  • Planning for the student you wish you were, not the one you are.
  • Colour-coding the plan instead of studying.
  • Starting with the topics you already like.
  • Skipping sleep. A plan that needs all-nighters is a broken plan.

A simple spaced-review schedule

Revisit a topic shortly after learning it, then again at growing intervals. A common pattern:

ReviewWhenHow long
FirstSame day or next day10 minutes of recall
SecondAbout 3 days later10 minutes
ThirdAbout a week later10 to 15 minutes
FourthAbout 2 weeks later15 minutes, then practise questions

Ask your AI planner to place these review slots automatically around the main sessions. They're short, which is exactly why they're easy to skip, so give them real times.

Long sessions or short ones?

Short focused sessions with breaks suit most people for new material. Longer 90-minute blocks suit problem sets and essay drafting once you're warmed up. A mixed day works well: one long block for the hardest task, shorter sessions for review.

What exam week should look like

  1. Stop learning new material a day or two before each exam; consolidate instead.
  2. Do timed past papers and mark them honestly.
  3. Schedule food, sleep and a short walk like any other block.
  4. Plan the morning of each exam, down to when you leave home.
  5. Keep one empty block per day for the unexpected.

Using past papers well

  • Do one under timed conditions early, to find your weak spots, not at the end.
  • Keep an error log: topic, mistake, fix. Review it during spaced-review slots.
  • Redo questions you got wrong a few days later without looking at the answer.

Common questions

Can AI make a study schedule for exams?

Yes. Give it your exam dates, available hours and weakest topics and it can draft a spaced, day-by-day plan you then adjust.

How many hours a day should I study?

It depends on your load; a common approach is short sessions of 30 to 50 minutes with breaks, scaled to the time before each exam.

Is there a free AI study planner?

Fuidzy is a free planner with a free AI model included, no install or credit card.

How far ahead should I start a revision plan?

As early as you can; a few weeks per exam gives room for spaced review and catch-up days.

What if I fall behind the plan?

Use your catch-up day, and if that isn't enough, cut or shorten the lowest-value topics rather than cramming everything.
