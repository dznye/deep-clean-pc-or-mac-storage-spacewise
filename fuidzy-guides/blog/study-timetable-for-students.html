Study timetable for students: template, example and how to build one

By the Fuidzy team · Updated 2026-10-09

A study timetable assigns each subject a specific slot in your week, fitted around classes and commitments. Start with the fixed things, add short subject sessions, protect rest, and revisit it each week.

What a good study timetable includes

  • Fixed commitments: classes, work, travel, meals.
  • Subject sessions with a start and end time.
  • Review slots for earlier material.
  • Breaks, sleep and a free block each day.
  • A weekly catch-up slot.

How to build it in five steps

  1. Fill in every fixed thing first, so you can see the real gaps.
  2. List the subjects and assign each a weekly number of sessions based on difficulty and exam dates.
  3. Place the hardest subjects in your best-focus hours.
  4. Alternate subjects so you don't spend a whole evening on one.
  5. Add short review slots and one catch-up block.

Using a timetable generator or AI

A generator can place subjects into your free time fast. Give it your fixed commitments, available hours and subjects with priorities, and check the output for realism: no impossible days, real breaks, a free block. Then edit by hand. See our AI study planner guide for prompts.

Keep it alive

  • Review the timetable every Sunday and adjust.
  • If you keep skipping a slot, move it to a time you'll keep.
  • Delete sessions that aren't helping rather than carrying guilt.

The exam period

Replace evenings of free time with extra sessions, but keep one protected evening a week and sleep fixed. Shift review slots closer together and add timed practice.

Mistakes that sink timetables

  • Scheduling the same subject for three hours straight.
  • Not including travel and meals.
  • Overbooking every evening with no slack.
  • Never changing the timetable after the first week.

Common questions

How do I make a study timetable?

Fill in fixed commitments, assign subjects to the free slots based on difficulty and exam dates, add review and rest, and revise weekly.

What does a good study timetable look like?

One with realistic daily limits, varied subjects, short review slots and room to catch up.

Is there a free study timetable generator?

Yes, several tools and AI assistants can draft one. Fuidzy is a free timeline planner you can use to place it.

How many subjects should I study per day?

Two to three is common. Switching helps, but too many in one day dilutes focus.

What if my timetable doesn't work?

Treat it as a draft. Adjust the length, timing or order of sessions until it fits your real week.
